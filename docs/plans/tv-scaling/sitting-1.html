Why the button was dead — measured at your panel’s real width

The dashboard’s drawing code had hidden ceilings (contents stop at 1.5×, card heights at 1.6×, text at 1.9×), and your wide panel starts at 1.6×. So the label climbed and the picture was forbidden to follow. A second trap: an old TV tab can carry a stale size in its web address that overrules settings forever. Both go.

This sitting: the engine’s remaining dials

The core is locked above. What’s left are the knobs on it — what the limits are, who wins when two settings disagree, and how the button paces itself. Six questions, most important first. The ⭐ row is my recommendation with the reason; nod or push back, either is fast.

5

A TV is showing a chosen slice of the board and you turn its size up. Who wins — the slice or the dial?

Today the slice is fitted to the screen and the dial multiplies on top, so turning it up crops into the slice. That has to be a decision, not an accident.

A

The dial wins: the picture grows past the slice’s edges and the outer parts crop away. The slice is the framing at 100%.⭐ Recommended — “bigger” stays one promise everywhere; checked against how the region maths already behaves, so nothing saved changes meaning.

B

The slice always fits exactly; the size dial does nothing on a panel that has a slice chosen (it only affects whole-board panels).

6

Once panels have their own sizes, what is the old shared “display size” slider for?

Every panel you touch gets its own saved size. The shared one is left over.

A

It becomes the default for a brand-new panel only — the first time a TV opens the link it uses the shared size until you give it its own.⭐ Recommended — a new panel shows up sane instead of at 100% on a huge screen.

B

Retire it — every panel must be set up individually, the shared slider disappears.

7

How high can the size dial go?

Today it stops at 250%, which is part of why a far-gone panel couldn’t be rescued.

A

300% — enough to rescue any real panel we’ve measured, and past that the wall is showing three words.⭐ Recommended — computed from your own panel’s numbers: 8px words need ~2× on top of a 1.4× start.

B

Keep 250%.

C

No ceiling at all.

8

How big is one press of the step-by-step red button?

You chose walking over leaping — this sets the stride. Every press still shows the measured before → after.

A

Up to +25% of the current size per press — lands in 2–3 presses. ⭐ Recommended — big enough to see from the couch, small enough never to overshoot.

B

+10% per press — gentler, more presses.

C

+50% per press — usually one press, occasionally too far.

9

Should the top bar of the TV grow with the picture, or keep its own size?

The bar already has its own “button size” dial made for fingers on the panel.

A

The bar keeps its own dial; the size dial moves only the content below it. ⭐ Recommended — buttons are for the hand at the panel, content is for the eyes across the room; two different jobs, two dials.

B

One dial moves everything, bar included.

10

Add a one-press test pattern on the wall?

A button that draws rulers and sample words at known real sizes for a few seconds — so you can judge a panel from the couch, the way TV calibration screens do it.

A

Yes — small, and it reuses the measuring machinery that already exists. ⭐ Recommended — it turns “is this readable?” from an argument into a glance.

B

Skip it.
