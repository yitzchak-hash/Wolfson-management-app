Bubbles,
not stages.

Today every apartment stands at one place on a line, and the app asks the line where the job is. What if each apartment instead carried its own set of things that have to be done — and you ticked them off in whatever order the site lets you?

The same nine items. On the left they are a queue you walk, one at a time. On the right they are a set you finish, in any order, several at once. On screen they keep the name you already use: stages.

What you've already decided

These are settled. They keep their numbers for good, so we can both point at them later. The ones still open are further down.

The naming, plainly: nothing on any screen ever says "bubble". It says stage, the way it does today — the word your office already knows. What changes is that an apartment carries a set of them with a state each, instead of standing at one. Bubble is only our word, on this page, for how they behave.

Why the line lies

Three things happen every week that a single stage cannot say

Two things at once

The piping is in, the geves crew is booked for Thursday, and the condenser is still waiting on a platform nobody built. The app must pick one word for that apartment. Whichever it picks, two of the three facts disappear.

Not every apartment does the same work

A shinui apartment has extra items. A standard one skips them. On a line those items still sit in everybody's list — so they read as skipped when the truth is never needed here.

Some places are not apartments

The pool room on minus two, the gym, the lobby. Two or three things to do each, and none of them is "AC installation". Today they inherit a ladder built for a flat.

The honest version: the stage list was never wrong — it is a good list of the work. What is wrong is that an apartment is only allowed to be at one of them.

The idea in one object

What a bubble is

One thing that has to be done in one apartment. It has a name, a colour, a state, and — when somebody is booked on it — a person and a day.

The six states
To do BookedSun Doing Problem Done Not needed

Not needed is the one the line never had. It takes the item off this apartment and out of its count — so 6 of 6 done is a finished apartment even though the tower's library holds nine items.

A problem is no longer a separate red world. It is a bubble in the problem state, sitting on the exact item that is wrong.
Your question, answered

"Ready to start isn't a bubble — it's a stage. How does that work?"

You are right, and it is the sharpest thing anybody has said about this list. Two different kinds of thing have been living in it since the beginning.

Work — somebody goes and does it

Piping. Geves. AC installation. Registers. Thermostats. Condenser. Each one is a job for a person on a day, it can be booked, it can go wrong, and it can be finished.

These are the bubbles. They count towards the fraction, they fill a segment in the strip, a task can name one, and the worker sees them on his phone.

A marker — a state of the whole apartment

Ready to start. Job completed. Handed over. Nobody does "ready to start" — it is a fact about the flat: the builder released it to us, or everything in it is finished.

These stop pretending to be work. They don't count, they take no segment, no task can name one, and they never appear on the worker's "what are you doing here".

And Job completed answers itself

Once every work stage on an apartment is done, the app already knows the flat is finished — 8 of 8. So Job completed stops being something somebody has to remember to set: the app offers it ("everything here is done — mark it completed?") and the office taps once.

Same in reverse: an apartment with no marker and nothing started reads not released yet rather than sitting at a stage called Ready to start while nobody has touched it.

This is the part of your list that was quietly doing two jobs. Splitting it is what lets the fraction mean something.

Three ways in, in order of how often you'd use them

  • The tipus fills it in. You already put A2 or C1 on the apartment. That type carries its bubbles, and the apartment gets them the moment the type is set.
  • You adjust the one apartment. Add "Second condenser" to 47 because the buyer asked; take "Registers" off the pool room. One tap each, on that apartment only.
  • The whole floor at once. The bulk bar on the buildings page already updates many apartments together — add or remove a bubble across everything selected.

A bubble nobody put on an apartment simply isn't there. No skipping, no "not applicable", no ladder with holes in it.

7c · So what happens with the pictures?

You asked the right question — it is the one part of this that could go wrong. Ask for three pictures per stage and a man who finished four things is taking twelve photos before he can leave. He will stop sending them. So:

One set for the visit — never one set per stage

Three pictures to close, exactly as today. The closing screen just names what they are evidence of: "Pictures of Registers — finished today".

Those pictures are then filed against every stage he finished. Open Registers on apartment 47 in the office and the pictures are sitting on it. They also stay in the task's messages, where they are today, so nothing moves and nothing is lost.

Finished nothing? The pictures still go up — they become progress pictures on the stages he worked, which is worth having.

Before you worry about the size of it

The app is already storing this

When we built the crossed-off stages in the apartment window, every apartment quietly started carrying a little map: this stage is done, that one is half done. That map is a bubble board already. What it lacks is three small things:

  • a third state — doing — beside done and half done;
  • a way to say a bubble isn't needed on this apartment;
  • and the decision that that map is the truth, rather than a footnote under one "current stage".

Everything else already exists: the list, the colours, the Hebrew and Russian names, per-workspace lists, the order.

Nothing is renamed underneath

We did this once before with workers: every word a person reads says worker, while the storage still says contractor — because renaming the storage means a migration across every device and every backup, for no gain anybody can see.

Same here. On screen it says bubble. Underneath it stays stages. Your existing data walks straight in: every stage you have becomes a bubble, the ones an apartment has passed become done, the one it is on becomes doing, and the rest are to do. Nothing to re-enter, nothing lost.

Which also means we can put it back if you hate it.

The honest part

What it costs, and what gets harder

What gets harder

  • One word at a glance. A stage gave every apartment one word. A set doesn't — so we have to keep inventing one (the bubble being worked now, or the next one up). If that headline isn't good, the buildings page gets worse, not better.
  • Every count changes. The dashboard, analytics, reports, the TV, the widgets — they all count by stage today. Each one needs a decision about what it counts instead.
  • Teaching it once. Everyone in the office knows the ladder. They'd have a week of "so where is it now?" before the new picture settles.
  • The CSV import has a Stage column. It would take a bubbles column, or keep the stage column and turn it into "everything up to here is done".

What it costs to build

Realistically four or five rounds — a week's work, at the pace we've been going, and the buildings page and the apartment window would look right at the end of the first two.

It touches more screens than anything we've done, which is exactly why it should be built once, whole, rather than half-on-half-off. Two systems living side by side is how the app gets confusing.

You said everything at once, and I think you're right — with the word staying stages and your current list becoming the first set of bubbles, there is no half-way state worth living in. Every workspace moves on the same day, each with its own list.

My honest recommendation: yes, and for one reason. The work on your sites genuinely isn't a line — you have known that for months, and the app has been quietly asking your office to lie to it every time two crews are in one flat. Bubbles is not a new feature so much as the app finally storing what is true. But it only works if we keep one glanceable headline word per apartment; drop that in the name of purity and you'll hate the buildings page.
