6 · v2The notebook: one kind of square, spanning its days — and why there were two

You saw two things in a square: a solid card with "day 2 of 3" pills, and a dashed chip. Here is why, in plain words, and the fix — which is also what makes the four-days bug and the "already in the planner" ask go away.

Why there are two kinds of squares today

A solid card is a record the notebook keeps itself. When a job is dropped on a square, or the dialog places a task, the notebook writes ONE CARD PER DAY into its own storage, each pointing at the task. That is why a three-day task is three cards with "day 1 of 3 · day 2 of 3 · day 3 of 3".

A dashed chip is a task DRAWN from the task list — a task that has a worker and dates but that nobody placed on the notebook by hand (made from the Tasks page, the drawer's Add task, the phone). The notebook shows it so it is not forgotten, dashed to say "this one is not mine, it belongs to the task".

So one task can exist twice: once as the task, once as the notebook's cards. And that is exactly the two faults you hit:

· Unchecking non-consecutive left four days. The editor rewrote the task's days correctly (two days). But the notebook's four cards are its own records, and nothing removed the two that no longer match. You were looking at the notebook's copy, not the task.

· "This job is already in the planner" on every save. The rule today is: when a task with a date and a worker is created, if the job has ANY card ANYWHERE on the notebook, ask. It never looks at the day. Two tasks on one job on different days are perfectly normal, and the question fired anyway — then offered "add a ghost / move the card / skip", which are questions about the notebook's copy, not about your work.

Your screenshot, read (2026-09-15)

Yaakov's row, the week of 4 October: Sunday 4 and Monday 5 carry a dashed "Streter, D… · Installation" — a task with Yaakov and those two days, drawn from the task list because nobody placed it on the notebook. Tuesday 6 carries a solid green "Streter, De… · Installation" — a card the notebook stored itself, wearing the stage's colour. Same job, same worker, same stage, two records, two looks. Editing one could never move the other. After the build the three days are ONE bar, "Streter, Debbie · Installation → …", Sunday to Tuesday, drawn from the task alone.

The fix: one source of truth — the task's days

The notebook stops keeping its own copy of tasks. Every task with a worker and days is drawn from the task itself, as one bar across those days on that worker's row. Dragging the bar to another day edits the task's days; dragging it to another row changes the worker; the X takes the day off the task. Edit the task anywhere — the drawer, the phone, the Tasks page — and the notebook simply follows, because there is nothing to keep in step. The notebook keeps its own record only for a job parked on a day with no task yet — drawn as the same bar, one day long, no words. Free-words notes are dropped (star 4).

The ask becomes an overlap check. Only when the new task's days land on a day the same job already has work — same job, same day — does it ask, and the question is about the work: "Levi, Dana already has Yossi on Tuesday 16 (Piping). Add anyway · Pick another day". Different days, different jobs: no question.

Rules the build keeps

  • The builder draws the diagram's own rows. One row model feeds both the project page and the builder, so they cannot disagree about the floors again; the empty Ground / Commercial row is removed from that model, not hidden.
  • A merged row is ONE unit. The kept unit takes the positions of the others; the others become blank placeholders that do not count. Its number, name, stage, tasks and Drive folder are the kept unit's. Unmerge restores the placeholders as blank slots — nothing is deleted.
  • Nothing is renumbered silently. Moving a floor asks; merging keeps the kept unit's number; every write goes through the builder's Save, and Save shows the list of changes before it writes (as it does today).
  • The star writes one field — the apartment's plan link — and nothing else. A missing plans folder no longer means "no plan": the pane browses the job folder from its root.
  • Thumbnails are Drive's own (the same picture the plan-aspect check already reads), fetched per tile as it scrolls into view, never for a whole folder at once.
  • The notebook plus makes a real task through the standing drop dialog; the task keeps all its days, the worker's phone shows every day. There is no free-words card any more.
  • v2The task is the only record of a task. The notebook draws every task from the task's own worker and days; it stores nothing about tasks itself. Every existing per-day card is converted ONCE on arrival (its days folded into its task, then the card dropped), so nothing planned is lost.
  • v2A task carries its ON stage (a new field beside the when-done stage; absent means "the job's stage at the time", so every old task reads as it always did). Closing moves the job to the when-done stage, unchanged.
  • v2The overlap ask compares days: same job, any worker, any day in common. Nothing else asks.
  • Every new string is bilingual — the dialog, the breadcrumb, the merge ask — from the strings object, both presets.
