Wall units
  1. 1The week ribbon across the top: a chip for every week near you — two back, this week, three ahead — each with how many tasks it holds and how many are done, and a small green bar. Press a chip and the notebook goes to that week. Older… opens a list of months for anything further back.
  2. 2Last week, folded: one line that says how it went — 18 tasks, 15 done — and names the jobs that were not finished, so nothing left over gets lost on a Sunday. Press it and last week opens in full.
  3. 3This week, open, at the top — every time the notebook is opened, and on Sunday at midnight by itself, even on a TV left on overnight. Today's column is lit. Today brings you back here from any other week.

Next week

Drawn in full under this week, so planning ahead is one scroll down — no button to find.

The notebook keeps its size

It becomes a window that scrolls inside instead of a sheet that grows forever, so a long run of weeks never pushes this week out of sight. You can still make the widget as tall as you like.

B

Weeks that look after themselves

no more pressing + every Thursday, no more putting weeks away by hand

  1. 4Three weeks ahead are always there. The notebook adds them by itself, so you can drop a job on a day three weeks out without pressing the plus first.
  2. 5Old weeks put themselves away once they are more than two weeks back. Nothing is deleted — every task, card and record stays — and the ribbon's Older… brings any week back.
  3. 6The plus and the eye you use today stay where they are, for anything unusual.

Why this matters

Today the notebook only changes when somebody remembers to change it. Your board shows 11 weeks put away by hand — that is the work this takes off you.
