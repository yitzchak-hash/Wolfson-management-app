The Studio on the Tablet

The markup studio was drawn for a PC with a mouse and then squeezed onto a phone. A Galaxy Tab S10 FE with an S Pen is neither. This page shows exactly what it does today on that tablet, what is wrong by measurement, and a proposal for a third layout built for a hand holding a pen. Nothing here is built — the ★ items are my recommendation; answer by number.

1 · What the tablet gets today

Landscape 1152×720 — gets the PC layout two rows of chrome + a rail: 26px buttons, 17px colour dots tooltips floating over the sheet — a finger has no hover
Portrait 720×1152 — gets the PHONE layout a third of the screen is empty navy the sheet is 44% of the height "turn the phone" — on a tablet
Pen tray, landscape the tray floats OVER the plan you are drawing on
Pen tray, portrait empty space above… tray covers the sheet below

Measured on the tablet (1152×720 / 720×1152, pixel ratio 2)

34 / 35buttons under 40px in landscape — the zoom, pager, undo, Layers/Download/Print are 24–30px tall
17pxthe colour dots on the ink row. Android's own minimum touch target is 48px.
44%of the portrait screen is the sheet (503 of 1152px); 36% is empty navy above it
2 rowsof chrome plus a rail in landscape — 30% of the height before the sheet starts
0controls for the S Pen: finger and pen are treated the same; the palm rule is the only pen-aware thing

Why it happens: the studio has two layouts, phone (under 768px wide) and PC (768px and up). Upright, the tablet is 720 wide → phone layout in a screen with twice a phone's height. Sideways it is 1152 wide → PC layout, whose controls were sized for a mouse. Neither was ever drawn for this screen.

2 · The proposal — a third layout: tablet

Keyed on what the device can do, never on its name: a touch screen (any-hover: none) whose shorter side is at least 600px. The iPad and the Fold's inner screen get it too. One rule underneath everything: the sheet is the screen; the tools are furniture around its edge, never over it, and every touch target is 48px.

The S Pen becomes a first-class citizen

  • Pen draws, finger moves. Once an S Pen has been seen on this tablet, a finger pans and pinches and never draws (a switch in the dock, "finger draws too", turns it back on). Today the palm rule only guards 0.7s after a pen touch.
  • The pen's button already erases mid-stroke; it stays. The eraser end of the pen switches the tool to Eraser while held.
  • Hover. The S Pen hovers — the cursor shows the pen's width as a ring before it touches, and hovering a rail tile shows its name (today's tooltips are for a mouse).
  • Pressure is already in the ink; nothing changes.

What stays exactly as it is

The pens, the neat shapes, the eraser rules, saving and versions, tabs and the plan chooser, the pins, download and print — all of the machinery. This is a layout for the same studio, not a new studio. The phone layout and the PC layout are untouched.

3 · The questions — answer by number

1Which screens get the tablet layout?
  • Any touch screen at least 600px on its shorter side — the Tab S10 FE, every iPad, the Fold opened. One rule, no device list to maintain.
  • Only the Samsung tablets.
2Landscape: where does the tool rail sit?
  • On the side of the drawing hand, set once per tablet (right-handed → rail on the LEFT so the hand never covers it; left-handed → right). Samsung Notes and Procreate both let you pick.
  • Always on the left, like the PC.
  • Along the bottom, like the phone.
3Portrait: what fills the space under the sheet?
  • A DOCKED tray — tools, colours, width, see-through — always open, under the sheet, never over it. The plan is always fully visible; nothing floats.
  • Nothing — enlarge the sheet instead (zoomed in, you pan to the rest).
  • Keep the phone layout as it is.
4The nine-pen tray on the tablet
  • Slides up from the dock (portrait) or out from the rail (landscape) and pushes the sheet aside — the plan is never covered. Same pens, same sliders, just a home instead of a float.
  • Keep the floating frosted tray as it is on the PC.
5Finger and pen
  • Once an S Pen is seen, a finger only pans and pinches; a "finger draws too" switch turns drawing back on. This is what kills the accidental palm scribble for good.
  • Keep it as today: both draw, the palm rule guards the moment after a pen touch.
6What goes behind the ⋯ menu?
  • Plans, Layers, Download, Print, Neat shapes, the version rail and the tabs — everything you touch once a session. Save, undo, redo and the tools stay out where the hand is.
  • Keep Plans / Layers / Download / Print on the bar as labelled buttons (48px tall).
7Touch size
  • 48px everywhere: tiles 56, colour chips 36 with 12px gaps, bar buttons 48 tall. Android's own minimum; the S Pen is precise but a thumb is not.
  • The PC's sizes with the existing "button size" setting turned up.
8Full bleed
  • A tap on the sheet with the Move tool hides the bar and the rail (the zoom pill stays); a tap brings them back. For reading a plan, not marking it.
  • No — the chrome always stays.
