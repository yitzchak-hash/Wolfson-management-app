The Arrange feature

drawn with the board’s real tiles and menus — press the buttons, everything works

This page is for approval before it goes into the live board. The grid arithmetic running in the demos below is the exact arithmetic the real feature will ship with — what you approve here is what Arrange will do.

1 · RIGHT-CLICK ANYWHERE — THE SELECTION KEEPS ITS MENU

Four tiles are selected. The right-click landed on EMPTY board, away from any of them — and the menu still speaks for the selection, says how many, and carries the new Arrange row. Hover the Arrange row to see its little animation.

2 · ARRANGE — MESSY TO TIDY, ONE PRESS, STILL SELECTED

Seven things — five job tiles of the board’s own size and two sticky notes of different sizes. Press Arrange: they consolidate into one compact, symmetrical block, centred where the selection was, and they STAY selected so you can still move them as one.

tallest first · equal 18px gaps · each row centred · block lands on the selection’s old centre

3 · CUT & PASTE — HOWEVER FAR APART, THEY LAND AS ONE TIDY BLOCK

These four are scattered to the corners. Press Cut (they fade, “in the air”), then CLICK anywhere on the board — they land as the same tidy grid, centred exactly on your click. Copy works the same, leaving the originals behind.

4 · THE SAME THING INSIDE A GROUP

Inside an open group window: Ctrl+A selects every job in the group (already live), the right-click menu shows the same “X selected” + Arrange, and Arrange tidies them inside the group’s own surface the same way.

THE SMART GRID, IN PLAIN WORDS — THIS IS WHAT YOU ARE APPROVING

  • Even gaps of 18px between everything, rows and columns alike.
  • Tallest things go first, packed into rows; a row is as tall as its tallest member, and shorter members centre vertically inside it — that is the “smart by size and aspect ratio” part.
  • The block aims at roughly 4:3 (a touch wider than tall). Same-size job tiles come out as the near-square grid — nine tiles make three rows of three.
  • Each row centres within the widest row, so the block reads symmetrical, never ragged.
  • Where it lands: Arrange centres the block on where the selection already was; a paste centres it on the spot you clicked.
  • Everything stays selected afterwards, so one drag still moves the whole block.
  • Locked tiles and the four fixture groups are left out; a job inside the block keeps its own size — Arrange moves things, it never resizes them.
