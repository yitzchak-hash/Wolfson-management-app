Buildings, Plans and the Notebook Plus

Three behaviour changes, drawn before they are built: the building layout in project settings showing the real apartments and taking the whole screen; the plan chooser browsing Drive like Drive, with a star on the main plan; and the notebook's day squares adding a job through a proper dialog. Each scene is a BEFORE beside an AFTER; the red dots match the notes under them. The starred questions at the end are the ones only you can answer.

Nothing here is built. Say "build it" and this page is the spec.

Today the builder draws bare numbered squares in a strip of the settings page, with its own idea of the floors. After: it is the project page's own diagram, editable — same rows, same names, same colours — full screen, so what you are moving is the thing you know.

"at least I can see what I'm touching and when I'm moving"

  1. 1The builder's own "Ground" row. It exists in the layout model but the project page draws nothing there; it is empty and in the way.
  2. 2The lobby row is thinner than a normal row, so its one named unit is cut off mid-word.
  3. 3A square carries a number and nothing else — no family, no stage — so you cannot tell what you are dragging.
  4. 4The builder takes the whole screen when you open Buildings: a full-height sheet with the building tabs, Undo, Save and a close X. The rest of project settings is untouched; this one card opens big.
  5. 5Move a floor. Drag a cell onto another floor (or press "Move a floor ↑↓" with it selected). It asks about the number: keep it (the family keeps the number everyone knows) or renumber that floor and shift the rest. Nothing is renumbered silently — the same ask the builder already has for other renumberings.
  6. 6Every cell is the real unit: the number, the family name, the stage as a colour bar — the diagram's own cell, the Building Progress look. A blank slot stays a dashed square.
  7. 7Merge across the row. Select several cells (click, shift+click, or drag a box across a row) and press "Merge selected": they become ONE square spanning their positions, one unit, one job, one number. The lobby is one job, so the lobby is one square. "Unmerge" splits it back.
  8. 8The floors are the project page's floors, exactly: Roof, 15 and 14 wide, 13 down to 2, "1 · Lobby" (the ground floor and the lobby are the same row), then -0.5, -1, -2. The empty Ground / Commercial row is dropped. Every row is the same height, so a named lobby or basement unit never loses its text.

"those three dots don't work. It just lets you type a weird text field"

  1. 1The free-words entry: a bare text box in the square with a "⋯" that does nothing. It was the paper sheet's "Pardes 8 am" — but it makes no task, has no job, and reads as broken.
  2. 2The plus is hover-only and tiny; on a touchscreen it does not exist at all.
  3. 3Press a square (click or tap, anywhere that is not a card) and a big plus appears in it; press the plus and the dialog opens for that person and that day. A second press on the square puts the plus away.
  4. 4The same dialog the drop already uses, with one step in front: the job. Person and day are filled in from the square; the person can be changed to anyone on the sheet.
  5. 5The job search runs across every workspace — a Wolfson or Netiv apartment, a Job Board job — with the workspace tag on each row, and the forgiving search the header has (Hebrew, wrong keyboard, one letter off). The last row makes a general job in a workspace with no unit, exactly as the Tasks page can. Picking a unit from another workspace makes a task THERE and a card here — the notebook's existing cross-workspace card.
  6. 6Add the job creates the task (with its days, when-done stage, the memo or files) and one card per day on that person's row — the standing drop flow. "Just a note instead" keeps the free-words card for the rare "Pardes 8 am" — small and grey, no longer what the plus does.

Rules the build keeps

  • The builder draws the diagram's own rows. One row model feeds both the project page and the builder, so they cannot disagree about the floors again; the empty Ground / Commercial row is removed from that model, not hidden.
  • A merged row is ONE unit. The kept unit takes the positions of the others; the others become blank placeholders that do not count. Its number, name, stage, tasks and Drive folder are the kept unit's. Unmerge restores the placeholders as blank slots — nothing is deleted.
  • Nothing is renumbered silently. Moving a floor asks; merging keeps the kept unit's number; every write goes through the builder's Save, and Save shows the list of changes before it writes (as it does today).
  • The star writes one field — the apartment's plan link — and nothing else. A missing plans folder no longer means "no plan": the pane browses the job folder from its root.
  • Thumbnails are Drive's own (the same picture the plan-aspect check already reads), fetched per tile as it scrolls into view, never for a whole folder at once.
  • The notebook plus makes a real task through the standing drop dialog; one card per day, the task keeps all its days, the worker's phone shows every day. The free-words card survives only behind "Just a note instead".
  • Every new string is bilingual — the dialog, the breadcrumb, the merge ask — from the strings object, both presets.
