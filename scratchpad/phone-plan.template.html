"call it task messages… the gray should cover the add-note too… your message, send at the end, a tiny mic inline to transcribe, the big mic like WhatsApp"

  1. 1The heading reads TASK MESSAGES with the chat glyph, on both the phone and the office's apartment window (one drawing, as always).
  2. 2The grey panel wraps the composer too — the messages and the box you answer them from are one thing. Translated lines keep their small "Show original".
  3. 3The paperclip is a round button at the start of the row, inline — photos, files, the same picker as today.
  4. 4The box says "Your message" with the small dictation mic at its LEFT end (your correction): press, speak, the words appear in the box for you to fix and send — in whatever language the worker's portal is set to.
  5. 5The big navy mic at the end records a voice memo and sends it as one, WhatsApp's way: hold to record, release to send, slide away to cancel. The moment there is text in the box it turns into the send arrow — one button, never two.
  6. 6Every recording is transcribed, whoever sent it — the office, a worker, a memo on a pin, a memo on a stage note, a task attachment. The player shows the recording and, under it, what was said; the words then go through the same translation as any message, so a Russian memo reads in English for the office and an English one reads in Russian for Igor, each with Show original. Transcription runs on the server with OpenAI's gpt-4o-transcribe (the "ChatGPT transcribe" model) through an OPENAI_API_KEY you add in Vercel — folded into the same lookups route, key-guarded like the translator. It happens at send time; a memo recorded before this exists is transcribed the first time somebody opens it.

How a stage report behaves ✓ approved as written

  • The apartment was at Piping; he reports Registers & access panels and says it is finished. The apartment moves to that stage. Everything ordered before it (Piping, Drainage, Concealed units, Thermostats) is crossed off the way the stage list already crosses off earlier stages when a job moves on — nothing is written onto them, so the office's picker still shows which ones were reported (a green tick with a name and a date) and which are merely behind (a plain cross-off).
  • The photos go to the stage he reported, and only there. They hang off the stage-report task the answer creates, and that task is for one stage. Nothing is copied to the stages he skipped past. If the office wants pictures of the piping, the piping is a separate report.
  • "Not yet" on a stage marks it half done (the orange clock), files his note under an open report task, and — new — moves the apartment to that stage if it is further along than where it stood. Your example: piping, not finished, sent to the office → the apartment reads Piping with the clock on it, and the office's bell lists it.
  • A report on a stage the apartment is already past is accepted as a record ("he also did some piping today") and does not move the apartment backwards.

what has to be true for this not to break anything

  1. aCounting. Every total goes through liveAssignments, which today drops a task whose apartment does not count. A general job has no apartment, so the rule grows one clause: no apartment and a workspace → counted under that workspace. Dashboards, reports and the wall follow by themselves.
  2. bThe notebook. A planner entry gets an optional general beside jobId; the card drawing, the drop dialogs and the purge sweep (which removes entries whose job is gone) learn that a general entry has no job to lose.
  3. cThe portal. The list card, the calendar chips, the bell and the task sheet all render from apartmentId; each gets the "Work at Wolfson" branch. The task sheet has no plan and no pins for a general job — it has the visit list instead.
  4. dSearch and reports. The Tasks subject in the report builder gets a "Where" field that reads the apartment or the workspace, so a report can list the visits.
  5. eBackups. All of this rides inside contractorAssignments and contractors, which are already persisted, exported, imported and synced — no new store key, the audit stays green.

What I would build, in this order

  • 1. The header and tabs — logo centred and bigger, print gone, language into the gear, workspace chips gone, the workspace tag on each task card, every workspace's tasks in one list, Today as the opening filter.
  • 2. The calendar — Monthly first, the grid filling the phone.
  • 3. The map — the chooser squares with the per-worker "maps he may open" setting, the one-bar map with the name button and the building segments, the outline, the filters and hints removed, scrollbars hidden.
  • 4. Task messages — the heading, the panel around the composer, the dictation mic at the left, the big mic and the send arrow.
  • 4b. Transcription everywhere — the server branch on gpt-4o-transcribe, the transcript stored on the note / pin / attachment it belongs to, the player drawing it underneath, the translator taking it from there. Needs OPENAI_API_KEY in Vercel; without it, recordings play as they do today.
  • 5. Stage reports — "Stages he can report" per worker, Ready to start never offered, the behaviour rules above.
  • 6. General jobs — the model, the checkbox and workspace select, the "Wolfson" notebook card, the "Work at Wolfson" phone card, the "is this part of…" ask, the visits list. The biggest piece; last so the phone is already right while it lands.

Three things only you can decide

★A general job, closing it. I would let the office close it with no pictures of its own, because each visit already carries photos through its stage report. Or require at least one visit before it can close?
★"Is this part of…?" I would ask it every time he reports work in that workspace while a general job of his is running, with Yes preselected. Or file every report into the running job without asking?
★The desktop portal (a worker on a laptop). I would keep the same one-bar map there, with A1 · A2 · A3 plus an All segment, since a wide screen can show three buildings. Or one building at a time everywhere?
